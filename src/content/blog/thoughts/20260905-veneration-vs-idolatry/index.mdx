## 1. 圣髑敬礼本身是否容易导致偶像崇拜？

加尔文的立场非常强硬。他认为问题不仅是“有些人错误使用圣髑”，而是圣髑敬礼本身存在内在危险。他在《论圣髑》开头就说，拥有圣髑是一件“无用而轻浮”的事，而且很可能逐渐导致偶像崇拜；紧接着又说，对圣髑的欲望“从来离不开迷信，而且通常是偶像崇拜之母”。他的逻辑是：人一旦开始珍视、观看、触摸这些物件，就会逐渐给予它们本来只属于基督的宗教性尊荣。原文位置：Calvin, *A Treatise on Relics*, Gutenberg 英译本约 pp. 218–221，对应网页 lines 958–968。([Project Gutenberg][1])

Calvin takes an unusually strong position. His argument is not merely that some people misuse relics, but that relic devotion contains an inherent tendency toward idolatry. Near the beginning of the treatise he describes relics as useless and spiritually dangerous, arguing that the desire for them is closely associated with superstition and frequently becomes the “parent of idolatry.” His reasoning is psychological as much as theological: once people begin handling, honoring, and depending upon sacred objects, they are liable to transfer to those objects honor that properly belongs to Christ alone. Original location: Calvin, *A Treatise on Relics*, roughly pp. 218–221 in the Gutenberg translation, web lines 958–968. ([Project Gutenberg][1])

## 2. 天主教的正式回答：敬意并不停留在物件本身

天主教对此的正式回答非常明确：如果一个人认为圣像或圣物本身具有神性，或者把希望直接寄托在物件本身，那确实是错误的。特伦多大公会议明确说，圣像之所以受敬礼，并不是因为人认为其中存在某种“divinity or virtue”，也不是因为应当向物件本身求恩，而是因为给予圣像的敬意最终指向其所代表的对象。《天主教教理》第2132条沿用同样原则：“the honor rendered to an image passes to its prototype。”原文位置：Council of Trent, Session XXV, *Invocation, Veneration, and Relics of Saints and Sacred Images*；CCC §§2131–2132。([en.wikisource.org][2])

The formal Catholic answer is equally clear: if someone believes that an image or sacred object possesses divinity in itself, or places supernatural confidence in the object itself, that would indeed be wrong. The Council of Trent explicitly states that images are not venerated because any “divinity or virtue” is believed to reside in them, nor because favors are to be sought from the material object itself. Rather, the honor given to the image is referred to the person represented. The *Catechism of the Catholic Church* repeats the same principle in §2132: “the honor rendered to an image passes to its prototype.” Original locations: Council of Trent, Session XXV, decree on saints, relics and sacred images; CCC §§2131–2132. ([en.wikisource.org][2])

## 3. Calvin 最强的批评其实不是理论，而是普通人的实际行为

加尔文并不相信天主教所作的 latria（只属于天主的崇拜）与 dulia（对圣人的敬礼）这种精细区别，在普通人的实际信仰生活里能够稳定维持。他说，经验已经证明，人几乎不会只是“保存”圣髑而不给它宗教性敬意；随后他直接把跪拜、供奉、把圣髑放在祭台显著位置等做法理解为把属于天主的尊荣转移给受造物。原文位置：*A Treatise on Relics*, pp. 219–221，尤其 lines 963–968。([Project Gutenberg][1])

Calvin’s strongest criticism is arguably practical rather than terminological. He does not trust the Catholic distinction between *latria*—adoration owed to God alone—and *dulia*—veneration of saints—to remain intact in ordinary religious practice. He argues that experience shows people rarely preserve relics merely as memorial objects without gradually giving them religious honor. He then treats practices such as kneeling, offerings, and placing relics prominently upon altars as evidence that honor owed to God has effectively been transferred to created things. Original location: *A Treatise on Relics*, approximately pp. 219–221, especially web lines 963–968. ([Project Gutenberg][1])

## 4. 在这一点上，天主教其实部分承认 Calvin 的担忧

这一点很重要。天主教并没有说：“只要叫作 devotion，就绝不可能成为迷信。”恰恰相反，《天主教教理》第2111条明确说，甚至原本合法的宗教实践，如果人赋予它一种近似“魔法”的自动效力，也会变成 superstition。《教理》第1674–1676条在谈圣髑、朝圣、玫瑰经、圣牌等民间敬礼时，又明确规定这些敬礼不能取代礼仪，而且需要牧灵上的辨别，必要时必须“purify and correct”。因此 Calvin 对“民间敬礼可能滑向 magic”的心理观察，并不是天主教必须完全否认的。原文位置：CCC §2111；CCC §§1674–1676。([Vatican][3])

On this point Catholicism actually concedes part of Calvin’s concern. The Church does not teach that a practice becomes immune from superstition merely because it is called a devotion. CCC §2111 explicitly teaches that even otherwise legitimate religious practices become superstitious when people attribute an almost magical efficacy to their external performance. Likewise, CCC §§1674–1676 specifically mention relics, pilgrimages, rosaries, medals, and other forms of popular devotion, while insisting that they cannot replace the liturgy and may need to be “purified and corrected.” Calvin’s psychological warning about devotion becoming magic is therefore not something Catholic theology simply dismisses. Original locations: CCC §2111 and §§1674–1676. ([Vatican][3])

## 5. 假圣髑是 Calvin 最难反驳的历史攻击

加尔文大量篇幅并不是在讨论抽象神学，而是在进行一种近乎“宗教打假”。他列举不同地方同时声称拥有同一个圣人的身体部位、同一件基督或圣母遗物等情况。他讽刺所谓圣母乳汁的数量巨大到难以置信；也列举所谓真十字架碎片遍布欧洲的情况。到结尾，他甚至说，在如此混乱的情况下，一个人可能以为自己敬礼的是殉道者遗骨，实际上却可能面对盗贼甚至动物的骨头。原文位置：关于真十字架，pp. 232–233，lines 1021–1024；关于圣母乳汁，约 p. 250，lines 1083–1084；关于错误圣髑的结论，pp. 281–282，lines 1249–1251。([Project Gutenberg][1])

Fake relics constitute one of Calvin’s hardest historical attacks to answer. Much of the treatise is less abstract theology than a sixteenth-century exercise in exposing dubious religious claims. Calvin catalogs places claiming to possess incompatible versions of the same relics, mocks the extraordinary quantities attributed to the Virgin Mary’s milk, and discusses the proliferation of fragments of the True Cross. Near the conclusion he argues that, amid such confusion, a worshipper intending to honor the bones of a martyr might actually be venerating the remains of a criminal—or even an animal. Original locations: True Cross, roughly pp. 232–233, lines 1021–1024; Virgin’s milk, around p. 250, lines 1083–1084; mistaken relics, pp. 281–282, lines 1249–1251. ([Project Gutenberg][1])

## 6. 天主教对此最合理的回答是：假圣髑证明鉴定失败，不证明圣髑神学本身错误

这一点 Calvin 的事实逻辑是成立的：如果两个地方都声称拥有同一个完整的圣人头颅，两者不可能同时是真的。天主教真正可以反驳的是第二步推论：具体圣髑造假，只能证明该圣髑有问题，不能自动证明所有圣髑敬礼都是偶像崇拜。实际上特伦多大公会议已经要求，新的圣髑未经当地 bishop 调查批准，不得接受为公开敬礼对象，而且同时明确要求消除 superstition 和牟利行为。这说明宗教改革时期的批评至少促使天主教更加认真面对圣髑真实性和滥用问题。原文位置：Council of Trent, Session XXV，末段关于 “new relics” 以及 “every superstition shall be removed”。([en.wikisource.org][2])

The strongest Catholic reply is that a fraudulent relic proves a failure of authentication, not necessarily the falsity of relic theology itself. Calvin is logically correct that two contradictory claims to possess the same complete relic cannot both be true. The Catholic response concerns the next inference: proving that a particular relic is fraudulent does not automatically prove that all veneration of genuine relics is idolatrous. Significantly, the Council of Trent itself required episcopal examination before new relics could be admitted to public veneration and explicitly ordered that “every superstition” and improper financial gain be eliminated. Original location: Council of Trent, Session XXV, especially the concluding paragraphs concerning superstition and the approval of new relics. ([en.wikisource.org][2])

## 7. Calvin 的圣经论据之一：为什么天主隐藏了 Moses 的身体？

加尔文特别引用梅瑟的例子。《申命纪》34章说，梅瑟死后被埋葬，而且没有人知道他的坟墓在哪里。Calvin 将其解释为：天主故意隐藏梅瑟的身体，是为了防止以色列人把它变成敬拜对象。因此，如果连梅瑟的身体都必须隐藏，那么其他圣人的遗骸也不应该成为敬礼对象。他在开篇和结尾都重复了这个论点。原文位置：*A Treatise on Relics*, p. 219，line 960；以及 pp. 279–280，lines 1239–1240。([Project Gutenberg][1])

One of Calvin’s principal biblical arguments concerns the body of Moses. Deuteronomy 34 records that Moses was buried and that the location of his grave remained unknown. Calvin interprets this as deliberate divine concealment intended to prevent Israel from turning Moses’ remains into an object of religious veneration. He therefore argues that if even Moses’ body had to be hidden, the remains of other saints should not become objects of devotion either. Calvin repeats this argument both near the beginning and near the conclusion of the treatise. Original location: *A Treatise on Relics*, around p. 219, line 960, and pp. 279–280, lines 1239–1240. ([Project Gutenberg][1])

## 8. 天主教的圣经反驳：圣经本身并没有排除 God 通过物质媒介行动

这里天主教有一个相当有力的反驳。《列王纪下》13:21记载，一个死人接触厄里叟的骨骸以后复活；《宗徒大事录》19:11–12则记载，保禄接触过的手帕和围裙被带到病人那里，而病人得到医治。天主教并不是因此认为“骨头自己拥有神力”，而是认为圣经至少说明：**God 可以自由地使用物质、身体或与圣徒有关的物件作为恩宠的媒介。** 因此，梅瑟遗体被隐藏可以证明“圣髑可能成为偶像”，却很难证明“物质永远不能成为天主行动的媒介”。

The Catholic biblical counterargument is that Scripture itself does not exclude God from acting through material objects. In 2 Kings 13:21, a dead man comes back to life after touching the bones of Elisha. In Acts 19:11–12, cloths associated with Paul are carried to the sick, who are healed. Catholicism does not infer that bones or cloth possess autonomous supernatural power. Rather, these texts demonstrate at minimum that **God may freely use matter, bodies, and objects associated with holy persons as instruments of divine action**. The concealment of Moses therefore demonstrates that relics can become occasions of idolatry, but it does not establish that material objects can never serve as instruments of grace.

## 9. Calvin 对早期教会的论点：最初的基督徒埋葬殉道者，而不是展示他们

在书的结尾，加尔文回到初期教会。他承认早期基督徒非常重视取得殉道者遗体，但强调他们这样做是为了“decently to bury them”，让遗体安息等待复活，而不是取出来供人观看和敬礼。他由此认为后来的圣髑文化并不是简单延续宗徒时代，而是后期逐渐形成的偏离。原文位置：*A Treatise on Relics*, pp. 279–280，lines 1241–1244。([Project Gutenberg][1])

Near the conclusion Calvin turns to the early Church. He acknowledges that early Christians took great care to recover the bodies of martyrs, but emphasizes that their purpose was to “decently bury them” and leave them awaiting the resurrection, rather than exposing the remains for religious veneration. He therefore argues that the later cult of relics should not simply be identified with apostolic Christianity but represents a subsequent development that, in his judgment, became corrupted. Original location: *A Treatise on Relics*, approximately pp. 279–280, lines 1241–1244. ([Project Gutenberg][1])

## 10. 天主教真正的根本回答不是“圣髑很灵”，而是 Incarnation

天主教最终最有力的回应其实不是为每一块中世纪圣髑辩护，而是一个更根本的神学原则：**Christianity is incarnational。** “圣言成了血肉。”基督宗教并不认为物质与灵性天然对立。圣洗用水，圣体使用饼酒，傅油使用油，人的身体也被称为圣神的宫殿。因此天主教认为问题不能简单变成“物质宗教＝偶像崇拜”，而应该问：这个物质标记的宗教行动是停留在物件本身，还是最终把人带向 Christ？《教理》第2132条正是用这个标准说明圣像敬礼：“the movement toward the image does not terminate in it as image, but tends toward that whose image it is.” 原文位置：CCC §2132。([Vatican][4])

The deepest Catholic response is therefore not that relics possess some mysterious power, nor does it require defending every medieval relic claim. It rests instead upon the fundamentally **incarnational** character of Christianity. “The Word became flesh.” Christianity does not regard matter and spirit as inherently opposed. Baptism uses water, the Eucharist uses bread and wine, anointing uses oil, and the human body itself is understood as a temple of the Holy Spirit. The decisive question is therefore not simply whether material objects participate in religious practice, but whether the religious movement terminates in the object or passes through it toward Christ. CCC §2132 states the principle very precisely: “the movement toward the image does not terminate in it as image, but tends toward that whose image it is.” Original location: CCC §2132. ([Vatican][4])

## 总结 / Conclusion

我认为 Calvin 在两个方面尤其有力量。第一，他对中世纪假圣髑、重复圣髑和宗教商业化的攻击具有很强的历史正当性。第二，他提出了一个天主教徒应该一直认真面对的问题：**一个原本为了帮助我走向 Christ 的 devotion，会不会不知不觉变成我真正依赖的东西？** 天主教自己的《教理》第2111条和1674–1676条实际上承认这种危险。因此，不能仅仅回答“我们叫它 dulia，所以肯定不是 idolatry”；真正需要判断的是人的信仰实际上指向谁。([Vatican][3])

In my assessment, Calvin is especially powerful on two points. First, his criticism of fraudulent relics, contradictory claims, and the commercialization of medieval devotion has substantial historical force. Second, he poses a question that Catholics should continue to take seriously: **Can something originally intended to lead me toward Christ quietly become the thing upon which I actually depend?** Catholic teaching itself recognizes this danger in CCC §2111 and §§1674–1676. It is therefore insufficient simply to say, “We call it *dulia*, so it cannot be idolatry.” The real test is where the believer’s trust, hope, and ultimate religious attachment actually terminate. ([Vatican][3])

而天主教对 Calvin 最有力的回答也不是否认这些滥用，而是说：**abuse does not settle the theological question**。一个假的圣髑证明那个圣髑是假的；一个迷信的教友证明这个教友陷入了迷信；但这些事实还不足以证明身体、圣像或圣髑原则上不能成为把人引向基督的记号。双方真正的分界因此不是“有没有物件”，而是：**the sign points beyond itself—or becomes the destination itself。**

The strongest Catholic answer to Calvin is likewise not to deny those abuses, but to insist that **abuse does not settle the theological question**. A fraudulent relic proves that the particular relic is fraudulent; a superstitious believer demonstrates the reality of superstition. Neither fact by itself proves that bodies, images, or relics can never function as signs leading believers toward Christ. The deepest dividing line is therefore not simply whether religious objects exist, but whether **the sign points beyond itself—or becomes the destination itself.** 🟥
