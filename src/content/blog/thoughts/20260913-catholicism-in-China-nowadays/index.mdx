> 阐述一下天主教在中国的情况，着重说一下共产党在这个问题上具体实行的管理措施的现状

> Please introduce the nowadays situation of Catholicism in China, focusing on the Communist Party's "pretending to be open while actually maintaining strict control" on this issue.

## 有限而有条件的开放 | limited and conditional openness

中国共产党对天主教采取的，并不是简单的“禁止”，而是一种**有限而有条件的开放**。在中国大陆，天主教堂、弥撒、圣事、神父、修女和主教都真实存在，很多教友和神职人员的信仰也是真诚的。因此，说“中国没有天主教”是不准确的。真正值得关注的，是中国政府允许宗教存在的同时，也要求宗教活动进入一个由党国设定边界、进行登记、监管和政治引导的制度框架。

The Chinese Communist Party’s approach to Catholicism is not simply one of prohibition, but rather a form of **limited and conditional openness**. Catholic churches, Masses, sacraments, priests, religious sisters, and bishops genuinely exist in mainland China, and the faith of many clergy and lay Catholics is sincere. Therefore, it would be inaccurate to say that “Catholicism does not exist in China.” The more important issue is that, while religion is permitted to exist, it is expected to operate within a regulatory framework whose boundaries are defined, supervised, and politically guided by the Party-state.

真正的问题，并不是“中国人能不能相信天主”，而是**天主教会能不能作为一个相对独立的教会组织存在**。在很多国家，政府主要负责公共法律，而教会内部的教义、圣事、主教任命、神职培养和教会治理由教会自己负责。在中国大陆，国家的介入则深入得多，涉及宗教组织登记、神职人员管理、主教产生、宗教教育、网络福传、境外联系以及政治教育等多个层面。

The real question is not whether Chinese citizens are allowed to believe in God, but whether **the Catholic Church can exist as a genuinely autonomous ecclesial institution**. In many countries, the state regulates matters of general civil law, while doctrine, sacraments, episcopal appointments, clergy formation, and internal Church governance remain largely within the Church itself. In mainland China, state involvement reaches much further, extending into religious registration, clergy management, episcopal appointments, religious education, online evangelization, foreign contacts, and political formation.

## 宗教中国化 | Sinicization of religion

这种制度特征在中国官方天主教组织自己的章程中表现得非常明显。中国天主教爱国会和官方主教团不仅强调遵守法律，还明确要求拥护中国共产党的领导、社会主义制度以及“宗教中国化”。这与单纯要求宗教团体守法不是一回事。一个宗教团体遵守国家法律，是正常的公民义务；但要求其内部制度明确接受某个执政党的政治领导，则意味着宗教组织的制度自主性受到更深层次的约束。

This institutional structure is particularly visible in the statutes of China’s officially recognized Catholic organizations. The Chinese Catholic Patriotic Association and the official Bishops’ Conference do not merely emphasize compliance with civil law; they explicitly affirm the leadership of the Communist Party, the socialist system, and the policy of the “Sinicization of religion.” This is different from simply requiring religious organizations to obey the law. Legal compliance is an ordinary civic obligation, whereas requiring an ecclesial body to formally accept the political leadership of the ruling party places much deeper limits on its institutional autonomy.

对于天主教而言，这种张力尤其突出，因为天主教并不是只有个人信仰，而是具有非常明确的普世教会结构：堂区属于教区，教区由主教牧养，而各地主教又与教宗及普世主教团保持共融。因此，**谁有权决定主教、谁拥有教会内部最终权威**，就成为中国政府与圣座关系中最敏感的问题之一。

For Catholicism, this tension is particularly acute because Catholic faith is not merely a matter of private belief. It belongs to a clearly defined universal ecclesial structure: parishes belong to dioceses, dioceses are governed by bishops, and bishops remain in communion with the Pope and the universal episcopate. Consequently, **who has authority over the appointment of bishops and who possesses ultimate authority within the Church** becomes one of the most sensitive issues in relations between Beijing and the Holy See.

1957年成立的中国天主教爱国会，正是在这样的背景下出现的。它强调“独立自主自办”，目的之一就是避免中国天主教在组织、人事和教会事务上受到外国宗教权力的直接控制，而这里最重要的外部权威当然就是圣座。此后，中国长期形成了官方教会体系与所谓“地下教会”并存的局面。

The Chinese Catholic Patriotic Association, established in 1957, emerged precisely from this political context. Its principle of “independence and self-management” was intended, among other things, to prevent Chinese Catholic institutions, personnel, and ecclesiastical affairs from being directly controlled by foreign religious authorities—most importantly, the Holy See. This eventually produced the long-standing coexistence of a state-recognized Catholic structure and what became known as the “underground Church.”

不过，今天已经不能简单地说“官方教会是假天主教，地下教会才是真天主教”。2018年梵中签署主教任命临时协议以后，过去一些未经教宗授权祝圣的官方主教被方济各教宗承认，很多官方体系中的主教和神父如今完全处于与罗马共融的状态。他们举行的圣事是真实有效的，很多人的信仰和圣召也是真诚的。真正的问题仍然是他们所处的**制度环境是否允许教会拥有足够的自主权**。

Today, however, it is no longer accurate to say that “the official Church is fake Catholicism while the underground Church is the real Church.” Since the 2018 provisional agreement between the Holy See and China on episcopal appointments, a number of previously irregular bishops have been recognized by Pope Francis, and many bishops and priests within the official system are now fully in communion with Rome. Their sacraments are genuine, and the faith and vocations of many clergy are sincere. The deeper issue remains **whether the institutional environment in which they operate allows sufficient freedom for the Church itself**.

梵中协议本身也说明了这种“开放与控制并存”的矛盾。中国政府愿意与梵蒂冈协商主教任命，这可以被看作一种开放；但在实际操作中，中国方面有时仍然坚持先作出人事决定，再由圣座处理教会法上的问题。2023年上海主教沈斌调任事件就是典型例子。它显示北京愿意与罗马保持合作，但同时又不愿放弃对中国境内教会人事的最终政治影响力。

The Vatican-China agreement itself illustrates this coexistence of openness and control. Beijing’s willingness to negotiate with the Vatican over episcopal appointments can be seen as a genuine opening. Yet in practice, Chinese authorities have sometimes proceeded with personnel decisions first, leaving the Holy See to resolve the canonical consequences afterward. The 2023 transfer of Bishop Joseph Shen Bin to Shanghai is a notable example. It showed that Beijing is willing to cooperate with Rome while remaining reluctant to surrender decisive political influence over Church appointments within China.

“宗教中国化”也是一个需要区分的概念。如果它只是指天主教与中国文化结合，例如中国式圣母艺术、中国音乐、中国建筑风格、用儒家思想讨论伦理，这与天主教传统中的“本地化”完全可以兼容，利玛窦几百年前就在尝试这种道路。但今天官方所说的“宗教中国化”，通常还包含政治忠诚、社会主义价值观、习近平思想以及与党中央保持一致等要求，因此它已经不只是文化意义上的本地化，而同时具有明显的政治和意识形态功能。

The “Sinicization of religion” also requires careful distinction. If it simply referred to integrating Catholicism with Chinese culture—for example, Chinese Marian art, Chinese sacred music, Chinese architectural forms, or using Confucian concepts in moral theology—it could fit naturally within the Catholic tradition of inculturation. Matteo Ricci was attempting something similar centuries ago. Contemporary state-sponsored “Sinicization,” however, generally also includes expectations of political loyalty, socialist values, Xi Jinping Thought, and alignment with the Party leadership. It is therefore not merely cultural adaptation; it also serves an explicitly political and ideological function.

这种管理也延伸到神职人员培养和互联网。神父、修女和修生除了宗教课程，还需要接受法律法规、爱国主义和宗教中国化教育。与此同时，中国对网络宗教活动实行严格许可制度，未经许可的个人和神职人员很难自由地通过微信群、短视频、直播或网络会议进行系统性福传、讲道和宗教教育。这样一来，一个人可能可以在教堂里参加弥撒，却未必拥有同样程度的自由去传播、组织和建立独立的宗教共同体。

This management also extends into clergy formation and the internet. Priests, religious sisters, and seminarians may be required to receive education not only in theology but also in law, patriotism, and religious Sinicization. At the same time, China maintains a highly regulated licensing system for online religious activity. Without authorization, individuals and clergy have limited freedom to conduct systematic evangelization, preaching, religious education, livestreamed worship, or religious organization through messaging groups, short-video platforms, or online meetings. As a result, a person may be able to attend Mass in a church while having far less freedom to communicate, organize, and build an independent religious community.

## 真正的问题 | the crucial question

因此，地下教会实际上是衡量中国宗教自由程度的一个非常重要的试金石。真正的问题不是“北京有没有开放的天主教堂”，而是：**如果一个神父、主教或者教友团体因为良心原因不愿加入官方爱国体系，他们是否仍然可以公开、和平地举行弥撒、管理堂区、培养神职人员并与罗马保持联系？** 在这一点上，他们面临的空间明显要小得多。

The underground Church therefore serves as an important test of the actual extent of religious freedom in China. The crucial question is not whether Beijing has an open Catholic cathedral, but rather: **if a priest, bishop, or Catholic community believes in conscience that it cannot join the state-approved patriotic system, can it still publicly and peacefully celebrate Mass, govern a parish, train clergy, and remain in communion with Rome?** In this respect, the space available to such communities is clearly much narrower.

这也解释了为什么中国的模式与共产时期的波兰有一个重要区别。波兰共产党虽然长期监控、打压和试图影响天主教会，却始终没有成功建立一个完全受共产党领导的全国性天主教体系来取代真正的波兰主教团。因此，波兰教会保留了一个独立于共产党之外的全国网络，后来能够与若望保禄二世、“团结工会”以及社会反对力量发生巨大互动。

This also explains an important difference between China and Communist Poland. Although the Polish Communist authorities surveilled, pressured, and attempted to influence the Catholic Church for decades, they never succeeded in replacing the genuine Polish episcopate with a nationwide Catholic structure fully controlled by the Communist Party. The Church therefore retained an independent national network outside Party control, which later interacted powerfully with John Paul II, Solidarity, and broader civil opposition.

中国共产党似乎恰恰避免让这种情况出现。它不一定需要关闭所有教堂，因为更有效的方法是：**允许宗教存在，但避免教会成为一个独立于党国之外、拥有自己全国性组织、人事体系、传播网络和国际联系的社会力量。** 允许有堂区，但堂区必须进入管理体系；允许有神父，但神职人员需要登记；允许有主教，但主教产生要受到政治影响；允许与罗马联系，但联系必须处在可控框架内。

The Chinese Communist Party appears determined to prevent precisely this kind of development. It does not necessarily need to close all churches, because a more effective strategy is to **permit religion to exist while preventing the Church from becoming an autonomous nationwide social force with its own organization, personnel system, communication networks, and international relationships outside Party-state control**. Parishes may exist, but within a regulatory structure. Priests may exist, but clergy are registered. Bishops may exist, but episcopal appointments remain politically sensitive. Relations with Rome may exist, but within controlled channels.

## 一句最准确的话概括 | in one sentence

所以，如果要用一句最准确的话概括中国大陆天主教的处境，我会说：

> **有一定程度的敬拜自由，但没有完整的教会自主。**

So if I had to summarize the situation of Catholicism in mainland China in one sentence, I would say:

> **There is a degree of freedom of worship, but not full freedom of the Church.**

一个中国天主教徒往往可以进入教堂、跪下祈祷、念玫瑰经、告解、领圣体、参加主日弥撒。这种空间是真实的。但如果进一步问：教会能否独立决定谁治理教区？主教能否自由与教宗沟通？神父能否自由福传？教会能否独立培养下一代信徒？一个堂区能否拒绝爱国体系而仍然合法存在？答案就会变得完全不同。

A Chinese Catholic may often be able to enter a church, kneel in prayer, say the Rosary, go to confession, receive the Eucharist, and attend Sunday Mass. That space is real. But if we ask further whether the Church can independently decide who governs its dioceses, whether bishops can communicate freely with the Pope, whether priests can evangelize freely, whether the Church can independently form the next generation of believers, or whether a parish can reject the patriotic system and still operate openly and legally, the answer becomes very different.

因此，“假装开放，实则严控”如果表达得更严谨，可以改成：

> **中国共产党并不是假装天主教存在，而是允许一种经过许可、受到监管的宗教生活，同时严格限制教会独立的人事权、组织权、传播权、教育权和跨国联系。**

Therefore, if we want to express “pretending to be open while actually controlling tightly” more precisely, I would formulate it this way:

> **The Communist Party does not merely pretend that Catholicism exists. Rather, it permits a regulated and licensed form of religious life while tightly restricting the Church’s independent authority over personnel, organization, communication, education, and international relations.**

最核心的矛盾也许可以进一步浓缩为：

> **国家相对愿意允许个人相信天主，却不愿意允许大量相信天主的人形成一个最终不接受共产党领导的独立社会组织。**

The central contradiction can perhaps be reduced even further:

> **The state is relatively willing to allow individuals to believe in God, but far less willing to allow large numbers of believers to form an independent social institution that does not ultimately accept Communist Party leadership.**

而对于天主教而言，这个问题尤其不可回避，因为天主教信仰不仅是“我个人相信耶稣”，同时也是：

> **“我属于至一、至圣、至公、从宗徒传下来的教会，并与伯多禄继承人和普世主教团共融。”**

For Catholicism, this question is especially difficult to avoid, because Catholic faith does not mean only, “I personally believe in Jesus Christ.” It also means:

> **“I belong to the one, holy, catholic and apostolic Church, in communion with the successor of Peter and the universal college of bishops.”**

因此，中国天主教问题最深处从来不是“一个人能不能戴十字架”或者“星期天能不能进教堂”，而是一个非常古老、也非常根本的问题：

> **国家权力究竟可以进入教会多深？谁拥有教会内部最终的权威？**

That is why the deepest issue surrounding Catholicism in China is not simply whether someone may wear a cross or enter a church on Sunday. It is a much older and more fundamental question:

> **How far may the power of the state enter into the Church—and who ultimately holds authority within the Church?**
